## Original Idea

A quick trial of `ruff` against `origin/main` found ~2.5k findings, roughly 1k
auto-fixable. The current stack (black + flake8) misses a class of quality
signals ruff surfaces: blind excepts, mutable class defaults, function calls in
default arguments, unused unpacked variables, and stale `noqa` directives. Ruff
also replaces isort, is much faster than flake8, and its formatter can eventually
replace black — reducing the linter surface while increasing coverage.

The proposed approach was to add ruff as an **additional** CI check alongside
black and flake8, configure a permissive starting ruleset so CI stayed green,
bulk-autofix the mechanical findings, then ratchet the ruleset tighter over time
via a growing `select` list, and only remove black and flake8 at the end.

Corroborating evidence was added from PR #3244 (issue #3194): `[tool.isort]` was
configured and isort was a declared dev dependency, but it ran in neither CI nor
pre-commit, and flake8 does not check import order. A reviewer flagged
import-ordering findings that no gate would ever fail on, and running isort to
fix them reordered imports across many unrelated files — the accumulated silent
drift the idea predicted.

## How the Plan Diverged From the Idea

Three parts of the proposal were inverted during planning, each on measurement.

**The ratchet was dropped, not built.** The idea's mechanism — a `select` list
that grows across PRs — is the shape ADR-0064 had already evaluated and rejected,
with in-repo evidence: the `strict=False` xfails for #1991 and #1992 sat
green-and-ignored because nothing failed when the tightening stopped. The
replacement needs no bespoke machinery at all: baseline the residue with
`ruff check --add-noqa` and keep `RUF100` selected, and ruff itself fails on
growth, on a stale marker left after a fix, and on the last marker outliving its
finding. Per line, not per file.

**Adding-alongside was rejected in favour of replacement.** The idea's step 1 kept
black and flake8 and added a fifth tool. Measured, that makes the motivating
problem worse: flake8 alone costs ~34 s on the whole tree and is what forces the
ten-minute `git commit` timeout, while `ruff check` plus `ruff format --check`
costs about a second. Deferring the removal deferred the entire benefit behind a
cleanup that the rejected ratchet gave no reason to finish.

**The formatter swap turned out to be cheap, not expensive.** `ruff format` at
`line-length = 79` reformats a minority of files — the two formatters already
agree on most of the tree — so black displacement moved from "separate later
decision" into the first PR.

## What the Measurement Changed

Re-running the trial rather than trusting the recorded numbers reshaped the scope
twice:

- The single largest finding category was a phantom. `EXE001` fired on every file
  carrying the standard `#!/usr/bin/env python` + CMU copyright header — a file
  template, not debt, and roughly a third of the reported total. Quoting the raw
  count overstated the problem materially.
- Selecting rule families naively produced far more findings than the default
  ruleset, dominated by categories this project has deliberately chosen against
  (`PLC0415` function-local imports, `TRY003` message-only raises, `PLR2004` test
  literals). That inverted the config strategy: the `ignore` list, not the
  `select` list, is the artifact under review.

The strongest argument for the work was not in the idea at all: some `CS-*`
requirements have no gate under them, and the work is enforcement of requirements
the project already has rather than a new bar. Counting them took two passes,
because the first pass over-claimed. Ruff gives CS-02-001 (`SHOULD`, ungrouped
imports) and CS-13-001 (`MUST`, naive datetimes) a first gate, and widens
CS-23-001's existing gate — an AST test scoped to `vultron/core/behaviors/` — to
the whole tree. Two others were initially miscounted into that set: CS-03-002 is
already gated, because flake8 bundles pyflakes and reports `F401` today, and
CS-21-001 has no ruff rule matching what it forbids (`RUF012` reports mutable
literal defaults, roughly the inverse, and every `B008` finding is a FastAPI
`Depends()` default). So the accurate claim is two first gates plus one widening,
not five ungated requirements.

## Scope Removed by Checking Live State

The exception-handling cluster was initially planned as its own issue. Checking
`origin/main` after freshening showed epic #3329 already owned it: #3325 merged
mid-planning via PR #3338 (visibly moving the `BLE001` count), with #3326 carrying
the remainder. Ruff adds a detector there, not a backlog — so no duplicate issue
was filed, the baseline markers cite #3326, and the new sibling issue was narrowed
to the exception-adjacent rules those issues do not cover.

## Outstanding Question Recorded, Not Answered

`PLC0415` is the largest exclusion, and CS-05-002 calls function-local imports a
"last resort" while the codebase treats them as the norm. Whether the spec is
stale or the codebase has drifted is a premise question with architectural blast
radius, so it was filed as #3350 and cited as the exclusion's reason rather than
decided as part of a lint change.
