## Summary

Add a short decision guide (a how-to, or a FAQ entry linked to one) that answers a
question a new reader asks and the docs don't answer: *when do I submit a report
(`Offer(VulnerabilityReport)`), when do I create my own case and invite others,
and when do I create a case and then transfer ownership to someone else?*

## Motivation

The docs explain how each path works, but nowhere say when to choose one:

- `docs/tutorials/submit-a-report.md`: how to submit a report
- `docs/topics/case_lifecycle/case_initialization.md` and
  `docs/topics/behavior_logic/use-cases/propose-case.md`: how a case is created
- `docs/topics/case_lifecycle/ownership_transfer.md`: how ownership moves

`docs/about/faq.md` and `docs/start/coordinate-cases.md` don't cover the choice
either. A reader has to piece the answer together from three explanation pages at
level 300–400, and two non-obvious points are easy to miss:

1. "Create a case" usually means *proposing* one to a case actor service. The
   service creates the case and keeps its history; the proposer becomes the Case
   Owner.
2. Invitations bring people in as participants, not owners. A finder who creates
   a case and invites the vendor is still the one making decisions.

## Rough Approach

The question that decides it: **who should be the Case Owner?**

- **Submit a report** when the receiver should own the case. The receiver decides
  whether a case exists; the reporter joins as a participant, and submitting the
  report counts as agreeing to the default embargo.
- **Create a case (and invite)** when you are the right owner: a vendor that found
  a vul in its own product, a coordinator that holds a report and needs several
  vendors, or a finder who really means to coordinate the disclosure.
- **Transfer ownership** when a case already exists and someone better placed
  should now run it, for example a vendor handing a case to a coordinator once it
  involves several vendors. History, embargo and participants carry over; only
  the owner role moves; the previous owner stays a participant.
- Include a small table comparing "submit a report to X" with "create a case,
  then transfer it to X" (who decides whether there's a case, who makes the early
  decisions, what happens if X declines), and the rule of thumb: don't create a
  case only to transfer it straight away.

Write it with `write-docs` (likely a how-to page under the reader-facing section
from the site IA work), link it from the submit-a-report tutorial, the
ownership-transfer page and the FAQ, and check that every claim matches the
current specs (CP, CM-21, ADR-0041, ADR-0048, ADR-0148).

**Processed**: 2026-10-26 — implementation tracked in #5189 (enforce the active-participant transferee rule) and #5190 (the how-to guide). Docs PR: <https://github.com/CERTCC/Vultron/pull/5195>. Spec: `specs/case-management.yaml` (CM-21-021). Decision: ADR-0160.

The interview found that an ownership-transfer Offer could go to an actor outside the case and carried the case inline, which leaked embargoed content; an accepted transfer to a non-participant left the case with no owner. The maintainer chose to require an active participant as transferee, which makes "invite first, transfer later" the path the guide describes.
